## 2. Getting the Google profile approved: exactly what Terell needs (verification readiness)

**Grade: Moderate.**
- **What makes it workable:** Google allows a service-area business run from home, and snow gear is easy to show on video.
- **What's hard:**
  - the business is brand-new;
  - there's no snow in October, so we can't film a live job;
  - magnets are weaker proof than a permanent decal;
  - name matching is strict;
  - if the base is a Chicago home, there's a zoning question (§3 rules).

**Profile type: service-area business, address hidden.** Google's rules:
- hide the address;
- keep the service area within about 2 hours' drive of the base [S: Google 3038177];
- list at most 20 areas, and no states [S: ppc.land].

### 2.1 Terell's question: "Do I just need a shovel, or do I need to rent a pickup truck?"

**A shovel alone won't get the profile verified.** It also can't clear 40 sidewalks before Chicago's 10 a.m. deadline.

**A pickup truck isn't needed either.** What he does need:
- a registered business name;
- an insurance certificate in that name;
- a two-stage snowblower, pushers, a spreader and ice melt;
- **his own car** with business-name magnets, plus a small trailer;
- a branded jacket.

Plowing goes to a partner who already owns a plow truck.

**Does he drive?** For service, yes: someone has to drive the crew and the snowblower to every job. For the verification video, no. He walks it.

### 2.4 What Terell does, in order, before recording

1. **Pick one brand name.**
   - Make it distinctive: no city name and no stacked keywords. "Chicago Snow Removal Pros" is a suspension risk [S: Google 3038177; CLAUDE.md].
   - Use the identical name everywhere: LLC or assumed name, COI, magnets, jacket, cards, site, phone greeting and profile.
   - Prefer a name that also fits year-round outdoor work (§7).
2. **Register the business.**
   - Illinois LLC: $150.
   - If the brand differs from the LLC name, file an assumed name: $30–150, depending on where you are in the 5-year cycle [S].
   - Get an EIN and open a business bank account.
3. **Get the city license.**
   - Chicago home-occupation license: $250.
   - **Confirm eligibility with BACP first.** "Dispatch, for compensation, of any type of motor vehicle" can't be licensed as a home occupation [S: Chicago BACP].
   - Suburbs follow the village's own rules.
4. **Buy insurance.** Get GL ($1M/$2M), WC, inland marine and commercial auto (medians in §2.2) [S: Insureon].
   - Get it in writing that snow and ice operations are covered.
   - Get a COI in the exact business name.
5. **Set up an owned phone number and domain.**
   - Number: through the shared 6 Central stack. A Twilio number is $1.15/mo (dispatch plan).
   - Domain: $10.46/yr at Cloudflare [S].
   - Site: one page with services, prices and service areas. Same name and phone everywhere.
6. **Get the brand kit on site:** magnets on the car, jacket and shirts, cards, and a name decal on the snowblower (**Assumption**: a cheap vinyl decal, not priced).
7. **Buy the equipment** and keep it at the base, in the garage or driveway.
8. **Sign one real contract or paid invoice** in the business name. Cover the customer's details on camera.
9. **Create the profile.**
   - Use the exact name. Primary category: "Snow removal service" [S: Shiftflow].
   - Enter the home address, then choose not to show it.
   - Service areas from §5, 20 at most.
   - Real on-call hours, the owned phone and the website.
   - Mention the season in the description. Seasonal businesses are eligible [S: Google 3038177].
   - List only services we really do. No ice-dam steaming.
10. **Record only when Google offers video verification**, in the Google Maps app on the phone.
    - Daylight, phone charged.
    - Rehearse once without recording.
    - A gimbal is optional (it's on the config kit list).

### 2.5 The one-take video: 90–110 seconds, walked, no cuts

**Google's rules** [S: Google 14271705]:
- show three proofs: where you operate, that the business exists, and that you manage it;
- record live, unedited and continuous, at least 30 seconds;
- show no bank, tax or ID numbers;
- show no other people's faces.

For a home-based service-area business, street signs and landmarks near the home count as location proof [S: SEJ; Digital Shift Media].

| Time | Proof | Camera (walk the whole way) | Terell says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Location** | Stand at the corner street sign nearest the base, both street names in frame. Pan to one real landmark: a school, a park sign, or a CTA or Metra stop. | "This is [Business Name]. We're based here at [Street] and [Street] in [neighborhood], and we clear snow across [3–4 service areas]." |
| 0:15–0:30 | Location → Exists | Keep walking to the house without cutting. Show the house number (the profile keeps the address hidden), then the car. | "Walking to our crew vehicle." |
| 0:30–0:45 | **Exists** | In one move: close-up of the door magnet (name and phone), then the rear plate, then the hitch and trailer. | "Our vehicle, our name and number, and the plate." |
| 0:45–0:55 | **Manage** | Unlock the car with his own key fob. Open the garage with his own key or remote. | "I'm Terell John, the owner. I'm opening our equipment garage." |
| 0:55–1:20 | **Exists** | Pan across the snowblower and start it for 3–5 seconds. Then the pushers, shovels, spreader, roof rake and stacked ice melt. Point the camera at the jacket logo and pick up a business card. | "Two-stage snowblower, pushers, salt spreader, the roof rake we use from the ground, and ice melt. Our crew jacket and card." |
| 1:20–1:45 | **Manage** | Show three documents in the business name: the COI (thumb over the policy number); the signed seasonal contract or paid invoice (customer details covered); tomorrow's route in the CRM on a laptop or tablet, not the recording phone. | "Our insurance certificate, a customer contract and our route, all under [Business Name]." |
| 1:45–1:55 | Close | Walk back out. End on the magnet, then the street sign. | "That's [Business Name], serving [areas]." |

**Two don'ts:**
- **Never film while driving.** Illinois bans using an electronic communication device while operating a vehicle [S: 625 ILCS 5/12-610.2].
- Keep crew members out of frame.

### 2.6 Why Google would reject it, and what to do if it does

| Rejection cause | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| The name in the video or documents doesn't match the profile | One exact name everywhere |
| A plain vehicle: "A video showing a plain white van will not be acceptable" [S: SEJ] | Magnets on both front doors, filmed in the same shot as the plate |
| Only generic, consumer-grade tools [S: SEJ] | Show the full kit and start the machine. Use the Professional 28 if he can. |
| Weak location proof, such as unmarked roads | Open on a street sign plus a landmark |
| Cuts, pauses, or a pre-recorded upload | One continuous take, recorded in the app |
| No proof he manages it | Unlock the car and garage, then show the COI and the contract |
| Sensitive numbers or other people's faces on screen | Cover them; keep the crew off camera |
| A virtual office, mailbox, coworking desk, TJ's address or a borrowed address | The real home base only |
| City or keyword words in the name | The real brand |
| A service area that's too large | The cluster only: 20 areas at most, within about 2 hours, no states |
| A second, duplicate profile | One profile |

**If it's rejected:**
1. Read the reason.
2. Don't create a second profile.
3. **Don't keep changing the name, address or category.** Those edits trigger re-verification, and pending codes stop working [S: Google 7107242].
4. Fix the specific gap and re-record when the option returns.
5. If Google offers no retry, use its support and appeal path with documents: LLC, COI, license and invoice. Replies can take a couple of weeks, according to forum reports [S: Local Search Forum].
6. Keep selling offline in the meantime.

## 5b. Five-location plan (highest return, real bases only)

**The rule.** Every location is a real base where a crew and its equipment actually operate: an operator's home, or a rented garage or yard bay where the crew reports and the gear is stored, staffed during listed hours.

**Never a virtual office, mailbox, coworking desk or borrowed address.** That's the Premium Home Service pattern. In May 2026, the DOJ (with the Illinois Attorney General) and the Minnesota Attorney General sued B.E.S.T. GDR LLC, which does business as Premium Home Service. The suits allege:
- more than **15,000 fake Google profiles**, many at addresses of unrelated businesses;
- more than **7,600 phone numbers** routed to overseas call centers;
- fake reviews;
- work referred out to third parties;
- about **$79M** in alleged profit [S: DOJ; MN AG; Olshan; Hoodline].

It's an Illinois-based company, and WBEZ ties it to West Ridge, inside our primary zone [S: WBEZ].

## 7. Two and three steps ahead

**Right after launch**
- The first storm drives reviews. Ask every serviced customer the same day with a direct link. Never gate or buy reviews.
- Calls jump in January: 165,000 searches nationally in Jan 2026. Route capacity is the limit. The storm desk triages, and non-contract callers get the one-time-push price or the waitlist.
- February is ice-dam season (22,200 searches). Send those calls to the roofer partner; our crews offer ground-level roof raking only.

**Competitors**

| Who | Likely move | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| Snow Pushers (pre-sold passes; hiring shovelers at $18) | Pushes passes and city-wide ads harder | A dense neighborhood cluster and a promise to beat the ordinance deadline |
| Apps and marketplaces: LawnGuru (from $37), Plowz & Mowz, TaskRabbit, Thumbtack ($65–100) | Drive one-time push prices down | Sell the season and compliance, not single pushes |

| City-keyword listings ("Oak Lawn Snow Plow" ×2; "Ice Removal and Snow Removal Services") | Likely caught in suspension waves | Never copy them. Report only listings that are genuinely fake. |

**Platforms**
- **Google**
  - The Premium Home Service case points toward stricter verification for Chicago home services (**Assumption**). A real base with matching documents is our moat.
  - Service-area limits: about 2 hours, 20 areas, and no states since Jun 24, 2025 [S].

**Regulators**
- **Chicago:** sidewalk enforcement falls mostly on businesses, which helps sell storefront contracts.
- **Illinois Department of Employment Security:** ABC-test audits if anyone is paid on a 1099.
- **FTC and the Illinois Attorney General:** franchise and business-opportunity rules once operators pay us.
- **OSHA:** if anyone goes on a roof. Nobody will.

**Build now so nothing gets rebuilt**
- An owned domain and number on the shared stack.
- A CRM with routes, and a **GPS- and timestamp-tagged photo at every visit**. That log is our slip-and-fall defense.
- Review-request texts.
- SOPs: storm trigger, route order by deadline, salt rates, incident report, no-roof rule, subcontractor COI tracker.
- The attorney-reviewed contract.
- A brand name with no "snow" or city in it, so it can carry year-round services.

## 8. Risks and kill criteria

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weather and fixed costs.** A light winter means churn. A heavy one makes contracts lose money. Insurance is 92% of fixed costs. | Cap contracts at 15 visits. Charge $4/inch over 8". Price one-time pushes outside contracts. Add fall and spring services to share insurance. Get commercial auto quotes. |
| 2 | **Slip-and-fall and property-damage claims** | Confirm the policy covers snow and ice. Photo log at every visit. Residential sidewalks are protected (745 ILCS 75/2). | 3 | **Storm-day labor and safety:** no-shows, fatigue, roof falls | Two backups on call. Storm-day premium (**Assumption**: +$2/hr). Workers' comp. Cap contracts at crew capacity. Plow partner as overflow. Nobody goes on a roof. |
| 4 | **Verification fails, the name is challenged, or Chicago zoning blocks the home base** | Follow the §2.5 script. One name everywhere. Ask BACP in week 1; a staffed bay is the fallback. Sell offline so this season doesn't depend on Google. |
| 5 | **Operator Program legal exposure:** franchise, business opportunity, misclassification, the fake-local pattern | W-2 only in 2026-27. A franchise attorney before any operator pays us or uses our brand. A real base for every profile. No profiles where we have no base. |

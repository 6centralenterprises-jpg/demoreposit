## 2. Getting the Google profile approved: exactly what Terell needs (verification readiness)

**Grade: Moderate.** Google allows a service-area business run from home, and snow gear is easy to show on video. What's hard:
- the business is brand-new;
- in October there's no snow, so we can't film a live job;
- magnets are weaker proof than a permanent decal;
- name matching is strict;
- if the base is a Chicago home, there's a zoning question (§3 rules).

**Profile type: service-area business, address hidden.** Google's rules:
- hide the address;
- keep the service area within about 2 hours' drive of the base [S: Google 3038177];
- list at most 20 areas, and no states [S: ppc.land].

### 2.1 Terell's question: "Do I just need a shovel, or do I need to rent a pickup truck?"

A shovel alone won't get the profile verified. It also can't clear 40 sidewalks before Chicago's 10 a.m. deadline. A pickup truck isn't needed either.

What he needs:
- a registered business name;
- an insurance certificate in that name;
- a two-stage snowblower, pushers, a spreader and ice melt;
- magnets with the business name on **his own car**, plus a small trailer;
- a branded jacket.

Plowing goes to a partner who already owns a plow truck. Someone must drive the crew and the snowblower to every job, so yes, he drives for service. He does **not** drive during the verification video: he walks it.

### 2.4 What Terell does, in order, before recording

1. **Pick one brand name.** It must be distinctive, with no city name and no stacked keywords. "Chicago Snow Removal Pros" is a suspension risk [S: Google 3038177; CLAUDE.md].
   - Use the identical name on the LLC or assumed name, the COI, the magnets, the jacket, the cards, the site, the phone greeting and the profile.
   - Prefer a name that also fits year-round outdoor work (§7).
2. **Register the business.**
   - Illinois LLC: $150. If the brand differs from the LLC name, file an assumed name ($30–150 depending on the 5-year cycle) [S].
   - Get an EIN and open a business bank account.
3. **Get the city license.**
   - In Chicago: the home-occupation license is $250. **Confirm eligibility with BACP first.** "Dispatch, for compensation, of any type of motor vehicle" can't be licensed as a home occupation [S: Chicago BACP].
   - In a suburb: follow that village's rules.
4. **Buy insurance** (medians: GL $1M/$2M, WC, inland marine, commercial auto) [S: Insureon].
   - Get it in writing that the policy covers snow and ice operations.
   - Ask for a COI in the exact business name.
5. **Set up an owned phone number and domain.**
   - Number: through the shared 6 Central stack; a Twilio number is $1.15/mo (see the dispatch plan).
   - Domain: $10.46/yr at Cloudflare [S].
   - One-page site with services, prices and service areas. Same name and phone everywhere.
6. **Get the brand kit on site:** magnets on the car, jacket and shirts, cards, and a name decal on the snowblower (**Assumption**: a cheap vinyl decal; not priced).
7. **Buy the equipment** and keep it at the base (garage or driveway).
8. **Sign one real contract or paid invoice** in the business name. Cover the customer's details on camera.
9. **Create the profile.**
   - Exact name. Primary category "Snow removal service" [S: Shiftflow].
   - Enter the home address, then choose not to show it.
   - Service areas from §5 (20 at most).
   - Real on-call hours. Owned phone and website.
   - Description: mention the season. Seasonal businesses are eligible [S: Google 3038177].
   - List only services we really do. No ice-dam steaming.
10. **Record only when Google offers video verification**, in the Google Maps app on the phone.
    - Record in daylight, phone charged.
    - Rehearse once without recording.
    - A gimbal is optional (it's on the config kit list).

### 2.5 The one-take video: 90–110 seconds, walked, no cuts

Google's requirements:
- three proofs: where you operate, that the business exists, and that you manage it;
- an unedited, continuous, live recording at least 30 seconds long;
- no bank, tax or ID numbers;
- no other people's faces [S: Google 14271705].

For a home-based service-area business, street signs and landmarks near the home count as location proof [S: SEJ; Digital Shift Media].

| Time | Proof | Camera (walk; never film while driving) | Terell says |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:15 | **Location** | Stand at the corner street sign nearest the base with both street names in frame. Pan to one real landmark (school, park sign, CTA or Metra stop). | "This is [Business Name]. We're based here at [Street] and [Street] in [neighborhood], and we clear snow across [3–4 service areas]." |
| 0:15–0:30 | Location → Exists | Keep walking, no cut, to the house. Show the house number (the address stays hidden on the profile). Then show the car. | "Walking to our crew vehicle." |
| 0:30–0:45 | **Exists** | Close-up of the door magnet (name and phone), then the rear plate, then the hitch and trailer, all in one move. | "Our vehicle, our name and number, and the plate." |
| 0:45–0:55 | **Manage** | Unlock the car with his own key fob, then open the garage with his own key or remote. | "I'm Terell John, the owner. I'm opening our equipment garage." |
| 0:55–1:20 | **Exists** | Pan across the snowblower and start it for 3–5 seconds. Then the pushers, shovels, spreader, roof rake and stacked ice melt. Point the camera at the logo on his jacket and pick up a business card. | "Two-stage snowblower, pushers, salt spreader, the roof rake we use from the ground, and ice melt. | 1:20–1:45 | **Manage** | Show three things, all in the business name:<br>• the COI, with his thumb over the policy number;<br>• the signed seasonal contract or paid invoice, customer details covered;<br>• tomorrow's route in the CRM on a laptop or tablet, not the recording phone. | "Our insurance certificate, a customer contract and our route, all under [Business Name]." |

Never film while driving: Illinois bans using an electronic communication device while operating a vehicle [S: 625 ILCS 5/12-610.2]. Keep crew members out of frame.

### 2.6 Why Google would reject it, and what to do if it does

| Rejection cause | Avoid it by |
|---|---|
| The name on the video or documents doesn't match the profile name | One exact name everywhere |
| A plain vehicle. "A video showing a plain white van will not be acceptable" [S: SEJ]. | Magnets on both front doors, in the same shot as the plate |
| Generic, consumer-grade tools only [S: SEJ] | Show the full kit and start the machine. Use the Professional 28 if he can. |
| Weak location proof (unmarked roads) | Open on a street sign plus a landmark |
| Cuts, pauses or a pre-recorded upload | One continuous take, recorded in the app |
| No proof he manages it | Unlock the car and garage, then show the COI and the contract |
| Sensitive numbers or other people's faces | Cover them; crew stays off camera |
| A virtual office, mailbox, coworking desk, TJ's address or a borrowed address | Use the real home base only |
| City or keyword words in the name | Use the real brand |
| A service area that is too large | Use the cluster only: 20 areas at most, within about 2 hours, no states |
| A second, duplicate profile | One profile only |

**If it's rejected:**
1. Read the reason.
2. Don't create a second profile.
3. **Don't keep changing the name, address or category.** Those edits trigger re-verification, and pending codes stop working [S: Google 7107242].
4. Fix the specific gap and re-record when the option comes back.
5. If no retry is offered, use Google's support and appeal path with documents: LLC, COI, license, invoice. Forum reports say replies can take a couple of weeks [S: Local Search Forum].
6. Keep selling offline in the meantime.

## 5b. Five-location plan (highest return; real bases only)

**The rule.** Every location is a real base where a crew and its equipment actually operate. That means an operator's home, or a rented garage or yard bay where the crew reports and the gear is stored, staffed during listed hours.

Never a virtual office, mailbox, coworking desk or borrowed address. That is the **Premium Home Service** pattern. In May 2026 the DOJ, the Illinois Attorney General and the Minnesota Attorney General sued B.E.S.T. GDR LLC (Premium Home Service). The suits allege:
- more than **15,000 fake Google profiles**, many at addresses belonging to unrelated businesses;
- more than **7,600 phone numbers** routed to overseas call centers;
- fake reviews;
- work handed off to third parties;
- about **$79M** in alleged profit [S: DOJ; MN AG; IL AG; Hoodline].

The company was based in West Ridge, inside our own primary zone [S: WBEZ].

- Calls jump in January (165,000 nationally in Jan 2026). Route capacity is the constraint. The storm desk triages, and non-contract callers get the one-time-push price or the waitlist.
- In February, ice-dam searches peak (22,200). Send those calls to the roofer partner. Our crews offer ground-level roof raking only.

## 8. Risks and kill criteria

| # | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Weather and fixed costs.** A light winter brings churn; a heavy one makes contracts lose money. Insurance is 92% of fixed cost. | Cap at 15 visits; $4/inch over 8"; one-time pushes priced outside contracts; add fall and spring services to share insurance; get commercial auto quotes. |
| 2 | **Slip-and-fall and property-damage claims** | Confirm the policy covers snow and ice. Photo log on every visit. Residential sidewalks are protected by 745 ILCS 75/2. Contract terms with each party liable for its own negligence (P.A. 99-0889). No "zero ice" promises. Umbrella cover for storefronts. |
| 3 | **Storm-day labor and safety** (no-shows, fatigue, roof falls) | Two backups on call. Storm-day premium (**Assumption** +$2/hr). Workers' comp. Cap contracts at what the crews can do. Plow partner as overflow. Nobody on roofs. |
| 4 | **Verification fails, the name is challenged, or Chicago zoning blocks the home base** | Follow the §2.5 script. One name everywhere. BACP check in week 1; a staffed bay as the fallback. Sell offline so this season doesn't depend on Google. |
| 5 | **Operator Program legal exposure** (franchise, business opportunity, misclassification, the fake-local pattern) | W-2 only in 2026-27. A franchise attorney before any operator pays us or uses our brand. A real base per profile. No profiles where we have no base. |

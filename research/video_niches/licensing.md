# Licensing and GBP eligibility for the video niches (2026-10-04)

"Unconfirmed" means I could not verify the point.

1. **Loctician (IL):** A license is required. "Hair braiding" includes "locking" (225 ILCS 410/3E-1), so locs work needs an IDFPR Hair Braider license (300 school hours plus the school's final exam; new applicants since 1/1/2026 also need a melanoma course). Mobile work is legal: give each client a certificate of identification (1-10), and a mobile salon needs one IDFPR salon registration. A home studio needs its own registration plus local zoning approval; whether Chicago allows one is unconfirmed. https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/core/dpr/258-qualifications-for-licensure.pdf · https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/core/dpr/189-qualifications-for-licensure.pdf

2. **Makeup artist (IL):** A license is required. Esthetics includes "applying make-up or eyelashes to any person" (3A-1), so the artist needs an esthetician license (750 hours) or a cosmetology license. The only exemption found is for film, TV and stage crews (1-11(c)); there is none for wedding or event makeup. https://lig.ilga.gov/legislation/ilcs/documents/022504100K3A-1.htm · https://idfpr.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/idfpr/core/dpr/131-qualifications-for-licensure.pdf

3. **Nanny agency (IL):** A Private Employment Agency license from the Illinois Department of Labor is required. Babysitter and domestic agencies are explicitly covered. It takes a $5,000 bond and a $250–$500 yearly fee, and the agency cannot charge a placement fee until the applicant accepts a job. https://labor.illinois.gov/faqs/private-employment-agency-faq.html

4. **Personal trainers:** No state license; according to NASM (July 2026), no US state requires one. The standard is an NCCA-accredited certification (NASM, ACE, ACSM or NSCA), CPR/AED and professional liability insurance, and insurers usually require the accredited certification. https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/blog/certification/do-you-need-a-license-to-be-a-personal-trainer-state-by-state-rules

5. **Translation:**
   - **USCIS:** a full English translation plus the translator's statement that it is complete and accurate and that they are competent (8 CFR 103.2(b)(3)). No notary or approved list.
   - **Illinois courts:** translator-certified; notarized only if the court asks. IDFPR's own applications require notarized translations.
   - **Agency license:** none found in Illinois (unconfirmed).
   - **Subcontracting:** normal. BLS reports 28% of translators are self-employed and that companies match freelancers to jobs.

   https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-1-part-e-chapter-6 · https://www.illinoislegalaid.org/node/189261 · https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/interpreters-and-translators.htm

6. **Call centers:**
   - **Telemarketing Sales Rule:** covers outbound telemarketing and third-party call centers that sell for a client. Calls customers start on their own and most calls answering an ad are exempt, but upsells are covered (16 CFR 310.6).
   - **TCPA:** autodialed or prerecorded calls to cell phones need consent (written if marketing). Sales calls are limited to 8am–9pm and must honor do-not-call lists (47 CFR 64.1200). So inbound answering and dispatch is lightly regulated; outbound is not.
   - **GBP:** Google doesn't name call centers. My reading of its in-person rule is that only a staffed, signed office where customers can visit qualifies. An answering service can't be a service-area business, and virtual offices are ineligible.

   https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-telemarketing-sales-rule · https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/64.1200 · https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177

7. **Carports (IL/TX):**
   - **No state GC license** in either state. Illinois licenses roofing and plumbing; whether a carport roof triggers the roofing license is unconfirmed.
   - **Chicago:** a building permit plus a city GC license (Class E needs $1M insurance) unless the owner-occupant exception applies.
   - **Illinois law:** jobs over $1,000 need a written contract and the consumer-rights pamphlet, and contractors must carry liability insurance.
   - **Houston:** carports need a permit.

   https://news.wttw.com/2025/05/27/following-recent-storms-illinois-attorney-general-warns-residents-repair-scams · https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/guide-to-building-permits/home/help/faq/DOB/GC-license.html · https://www.houstontx.gov/planning/Publications/inform_brch/Permit-Brochure_Carports.pdf

8. **Phoenix patio covers and additions:** An ROC license is required: B for additions, R-3 or B for patio covers. The handyman exemption covers jobs under $1,000 including materials, casual or minor work, and no permit, and ads must say "not a licensed contractor" (A.R.S. 32-1121(A)(14)). Phoenix requires permits for patio covers and additions, so the exemption doesn't apply. Bills to raise the limit were proposed, but the statute still reads $1,000. https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01121.htm · https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/arizona/Ariz-Admin-Code-SS-R4-9-103

9. **Pool deck resurfacing:**
   - **Arizona:** an ROC license is required unless the job fits the handyman exemption. R-9 (concrete) and R-34 (which includes liquid floor coatings) are likely classes; R-6 excludes deck replacement and R-21 excludes deck coatings. The exact class is unconfirmed.
   - **Florida:** no state license specific to deck coating was found. Decking tied to the pool structure falls under pool/spa contractors, and counties may not require licenses for painting or pressure washing. Whether coating counts as painting is unconfirmed.

   https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/florida/Fla-Admin-Code-Ann-R-61G4-15-032 · https://m.flsenate.gov/Statutes/489.117

10. **Chicago yacht charters:**
    - **Up to 6 paying passengers** (uninspected vessel under 100 GT): OUPV "six-pack" credential, which on the Great Lakes takes 12 months of service including 3 on the Great Lakes.
    - **More than 6:** USCG Certificate of Inspection plus a Master credential.
    - **Bareboat:** at most 12 passengers and a written charter. The charterer picks and pays the crew, and the owner can't be aboard.
    - **Enforcement:** in July 2025 the Coast Guard stopped 4 illegal Chicago charters; penalties exceed $69K per voyage.

    https://wow.uscgaux.info/Uploads_wowII/P-DEPT/PaxForHire_trifold_3.pdf · https://www.dvidshub.net/news/542599/coast-guard-sector-lake-michigan-terminates-four-illegal-charters-over-fourth-july-weekend

11. **GBP (verbatim):**
    - "To qualify for a Business Profile, a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours."
    - Ineligible: "Lead generation agents or companies."
    - Service-area business: "A business that visits or delivers to customers directly but doesn't serve customers at their business address."

    https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036 · https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481

12. **Escape rooms and parasailing:**
    - **Escape rooms:** local fire officials may classify them as NFPA 101 "special amusement buildings," which brings sprinkler, alarm and fail-safe lock requirements. https://www.nxtbook.com/nxtbooks/nfpa/journal_20190708/index.php?startid=39
    - **Parasailing:** a USCG passenger-for-hire license is required. Florida also requires $1M/$2M liability insurance and wind limits; Illinois rules are unconfirmed. https://www.flsenate.gov/Laws/Statutes/2024/327.375

# Depot/Staging Locations and Brand-Name Options: Mobile Tire Repair (DFW, Houston, Atlanta)

Research date: 2026-10-12. Every listing below comes from a public listing snapshot. Small-bay units lease quickly, and several snapshots carry 2025 or early-2026 dates. Treat each one as a lead to confirm with the listing broker, not as confirmed availability. All addresses come from cited sources. None is invented.

## DFW: central submarket and a real small-bay listing

### Takeaway
The Great Southwest (GSW) Industrial District in Grand Prairie/Arlington is the most central small-bay option in DFW. It sits between I-30, SH-360, I-20 and SH-161, roughly midway between Dallas and Fort Worth. Two public listings on S Great Southwest Pkwy show units of about 1,500 SF with grade-level or drive-in doors at $12.00–$15.50/SF/yr.

## Zoning and fire code for tire storage and service

### Takeaway
Tire service and storage in light-industrial zones is generally allowed but often conditional. In Grand Prairie, for example, auto repair in Light Industrial goes through a Specific Use Permit. Under fire code, all indoor tire storage typically requires an operational permit, scrap tire storage triggers a permit above 2,500 cu ft, and tire piles over 6' tall covering more than 500 SF trigger high-piled storage permits. A mobile-only depot holding a modest tire inventory can usually stay under the high-piled thresholds.

### Cited Findings
- Grand Prairie: council and planning agendas show Specific Use Permit (SUP) requests for "Major Auto Repair" on Light Industrial-zoned property, including a 2026 request on Doreen Street. Permissible uses are set by Article IV of the city's Unified Development Code. — [GP PD 208 doc](https://www.gptx.org/files/sharedassets/public/departments/planning/documents/planneddevelopmentdistricts/PD_208.pdf); [GP P&Z video index](https://grandprairietx.new.swagit.com/videos/393506)
- IFC Chapter 34 (Tire Rebuilding and Tire Storage) applies to tire rebuilding plants, tire storage and tire byproduct facilities, and references NFPA 13. — [Seattle Fire Code Ch. 34, 2021 IFC](https://seattle.gov/documents/departments/fire/business/fire%20code%20and%20safety%20documents/34_ifc_2021.pdf)
- Operational permit triggers based on the IFC: scrap tire storage above 2,500 cu ft requires a permit, as does *all indoor storage* of tires and tire byproducts. Indoor tire storage over 6' high covering more than 500 SF also needs a high-piled storage permit. — [City of Vancouver, WA VFD permit app citing WSFC 105.5.48](https://cityofvancouver.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Fillable-VFD-Scrap-Tire-Storage-Permit-Application-OPS.pdf); [Manteca, CA](https://manteca.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/4940/637915159191570000)
- The City of Houston uses the same 2,500 cu ft scrap tire threshold. — [Houston Permitting Center doc](https://www.houstonpermittingcenter.org/media/7556/download)
- States amend these limits. Ohio, for example, sets a 2,500 SF pile area limit and a 20,000 cu ft volume cap, against the model code's 50,000. — [Ohio Admin Code 1301:7-7-34](https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-administrative-code/rule-1301:7-7-34)

### Inferences
- An operation that only uses the depot to park vans, stock plugs, patches and TPMS parts, and hold a small rack of new tires, should stay below the 6' / 500 SF high-piled trigger. It may still need a local operational permit for indoor tire storage, and Texas and Georgia generally require scrap-tire hauling and manifest compliance for used tires.
- Before signing a lease, get a zoning verification letter for the specific address, and confirm with the fire marshal that indoor tire storage is allowed.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve Atlanta, South Fulton or Doraville zoning use tables. I also did not research Texas TCEQ or Georgia EPD scrap-tire generator and hauler rules.

## Brand names: candidates and conflict checks

### Takeaway
Brandable names with a light descriptor, such as "[Brand] Mobile Tire," avoid conflicts better than generic phrases. Several obvious phrases are already taken as .com domains (TireRescue, FlatPatrol, FlatFixers, CurbsideTire, TreadRunner). Names that tie into the TJ's brand family, such as "TJ's Tire Rescue," showed no web conflict and no DNS on .com.

### Cited Findings
- I ran DNS lookups on 2026-10-12. "NO_DNS" means the domain did not resolve. That suggests the domain may be unregistered, but it does not prove it; registrar WHOIS is needed to confirm.
  - No DNS (possibly available): tjstirerescue.com, tjsmobiletire.com, centraltirerescue.com, rollreadytire.com, patchandroll.com
  - Resolves (registered/in use): tirerescue.com, flatpatrol.com, flatfixers.com, curbsidetire.com, treadrunner.com, tirefixnow.com, rolltire.com
  - Source: DNS lookups (`getent hosts`) run in this session; no URL.
- Web search found no business named "TJ's Tire Rescue" or "TJs Mobile Tire." Similar-sounding existing names include TJ's Tire & Auto Repair LLC (River Edge, NJ), TJ's Shell Station LLC (Raceland, LA) and Tj's Tire and Auto, a Goodyear dealer in Unity, WI. None of these is mobile, and none is in TX or GA. — [SimpleTire TJ's Tire & Auto](https://simpletire.com/tire-shops/shop/tjs-tire-auto-repair-llc/78855); [Goodyear Tj's Tire and Auto](https://www.goodyear.com/en-us/shops/WI/Unity/Tj-S-Tire-And-Auto-000020478)
- "TIRE TAXI" is a filed trademark owned by Mobile Fleet Medic LLC (filed February 1, 2022) covering tire repair and emergency roadside repair. Avoid it. — [Justia Trademarks](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/mobile-fleet-medic-llc-5093652)
- "TIRE RUNNER" is a registered mark (Reg. No. 4631931, Section 8 & 15 accepted) covering tire installation and repair. It is close enough to create risk for "TreadRunner." — [Justia TIRE RUNNER](https://trademarks.justia.com/862/29/tire-86229269.html)
- Goodyear runs a mobile tire installation service called "Roll by Goodyear," so names built on "Roll" carry confusion risk with a major brand. — [The Drive](https://thedrive.com/news/24436/goodyear-starts-streamlined-tire-installation-service-in-maryland)
- Search found no company operating as "Tire Rescue," "TireDash," "Flat Patrol" or "Tire Medic." The .com domains for several of these are still registered by someone. — search results; DNS check above

### Inferences
Candidate shortlist, ranked:
1. **TJ's Tire Rescue**: extends the existing "TJ's Nationwide Roadside" brand, no web conflict found, and tjstirerescue.com did not resolve. Good for cross-selling and shared reviews.
2. **TJ's Mobile Tire**: plain and descriptive, ranks well for "mobile tire" searches, and tjsmobiletire.com did not resolve.
3. **Central Tire Rescue**: ties to 6 Central Enterprises, and centraltirerescue.com did not resolve. "Central" is generic, which makes a trademark weaker.
4. **Patch & Roll Mobile Tire**: memorable and brandable, and patchandroll.com did not resolve. "Roll" has slight overlap with Roll by Goodyear.
5. **Roll Ready Mobile Tire**: rollreadytire.com did not resolve, but it shares the same Goodyear "Roll" caution.
6. **Flat Patrol** / **Tire Rescue**: strong concepts, but the .com domains are taken. They would need a city suffix such as flatpatroldfw.com, and conflicts with the current holders are unchecked.

- Local SEO: Google Business Profile guidelines prohibit keyword-stuffing the business name, so the legal or DBA name should actually contain any descriptor you want shown. A name like "TJ's Mobile Tire" legitimately includes "mobile tire," which tends to help map-pack relevance. City names are better used in per-metro landing pages and GBP service areas (for example /dallas-mobile-tire-repair) than in the brand itself, which has to work across three metros. This is an inference and was not sourced in this pass.

### Gaps
- I did not run a direct USPTO search (tmsearch.uspto.gov). Search-engine coverage of USPTO records is incomplete, so every finalist needs a direct USPTO check plus Texas SOS and Georgia SOS entity searches.
- DNS non-resolution does not prove a .com is available. Confirm through a registrar.
